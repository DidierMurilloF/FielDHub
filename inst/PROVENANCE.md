# Provenance of the compiled block engine

The engine is a clean-room implementation. Nobody working on it read the source
code of the blocksdesign package; blocksdesign is used only as an installed
black box in tests and benchmarks, to compare output shape and efficiency.

| File | Content | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| `block_space.h`, `block_space.cpp` | Block-space information matrix, swap ratio, Woodbury update, D- and A-efficiency | Edmondson (2020), JABES 25:500-522, sections 2.1-2.3 (model, conditional swaps, D-optimality); the matrix determinant lemma, Woodbury and Sylvester identities; research derivation `derivation.md` (gate G0, 4 Nov 2026). The cached ratio (`enable_cache()`, `delta_cached()`: G = Minv N'/r, updated by rank 2) is FielDHub's own rearrangement of the same ratio (spec-m5 section 6.5) |
| `blocks_engine.cpp` | Randomized first-improvement swap search, escapes, budget | Edmondson (2020) section 2.2; Mitchell (1974), Technometrics 16:203-210 (exchange search); spec-v2 sections 6, 8, 9 |

| `blocks_engine.cpp` (`even_repair()`) | Even-up repair of the chosen design: swaps that leave every treatment-block count within one of the others | FielDHub's own rule (spec-m5 section 6.1): each swap lowers the sum of squared counts by at least 2 and a partner always exists (counting argument); candidates are scored with the swap ratio above; `do_optim()` is the engine's first caller |
| `blocks_engine.cpp` (`lattice_start()`) | Square-lattice start for the first search when v = q^2 and the blocks hold q plots | Textbook construction (Yates 1936): rows, columns and the Latin squares (i + m j) mod q, orthogonal for up to three replicates at any q and q + 1 at prime q; labels randomly permuted (spec-m5 section 6.4) |
| `block_space_probe.cpp` | Test hook that replays swaps on the block-space state | spec-v2 section 12 |
| `engine_budget.h` | Evaluation budget, interrupts, time limit | spec-v2 sections 6 and 9 |
| `R/engine_blocks.R` (`block_a_bound()`) | Convexity bound on A-efficiency | John & Williams (1995), *Cyclic and Computer Generated Designs*, ch. 2 (efficiency factors); own derivation (plan review gate) |

| `R/engine_lattice_constructions.R` (`mols()`, `mols_max()`) | q - 1 mutually orthogonal Latin squares L_a(x, y) = a x + y over GF(q), a nonzero, for prime powers q; at other orders the MacNeish count, raised to 2 by the order 3m + 1 pair, a stored pair or a product of such pairs (rows below) | Bose (1938), Sankhya 3:323-338; Raghavarao (1971) section 1.3 |
| `R/engine_lattice_constructions.R` (`prime_power_factors()`, `direct_product_mols()`, `product_split()`) | At a composite order q = q_1 q_2 ... The same product over any factorisation q = m n with two squares of each order, whatever their source (N(m n) >= min(N(m), N(n))): order 30 = 3 x 10 | MacNeish (1922), Ann. Math. 23:221-227; van Lint & Wilson, *A Course in Combinatorics*, ch. 22 (direct product of orthogonal arrays); nested-engine spec section 6.5 |
| `R/engine_lattice_constructions.R` (`three_m_plus_one_order()`, `three_m_plus_one()`) | Two orthogonal Latin squares of order q = 3m + 1, m not 2 or 6, from an OA(4, q): base columns (inf_i, 0, i, 2i), (0, inf_i, 2i, i), (i, 2i, inf_i, 0), (2i, i, 0, inf_i) developed over Z_(2m+1) with inf_i fixed, the 2m + 1 constant columns, and the m^2 hole columns from two orthogonal squares of order m; rows 1-2 index the cell, rows 3-4 the squares | Bose, Shrikhande & Parker (1960), Canad. J. Math. 12:189-203; van Lint & Wilson, *A Course in Combinatorics*, ch. 22 (quasi-difference matrices); nested-engine spec section 6.5 (recipe as written there) |
| `R/engine_lattice_constructions.R` (`lattice_classes()`, `resolvable_lattice_start()`) | Square lattice of order q with r <= q + 1 replicates: treatment i q + j in group i, group j and group L_m(i, j); labels and block order permuted; recognised from (v, b) alone, no random draws when there is no construction | Yates (1936), J. Agric. Sci. 26:424-455 (lattice designs); Bose (1938); nested-engine spec section 6.5 |
| `R/engine_lattice_constructions.R` (`square_lattice_efficiencies()`), `R/engine_blocks.R` (`unsearched_resolvable_design()`, stop reason "construction") | Closed-form Level 2 efficiencies of a square lattice (efficiency factors (r - 1) / r on the r (q - 1) between-group contrasts, 1 elsewhere); the construction returned unsearched and unscored when the work-based admission refuses the shape | Own derivation from C = r I - N N' / q and the orthogonality of the replicates' group spaces; nested-engine spec section 6.2b decision 4 |
| `R/engine_blocks.R` (`resolvable_a_bound()`, `unsearched_resolvable_design()`), `blocks_engine.cpp` (`resolvable_score_cpp()`) | Resolvable A-efficiency bound: the r replicates make rank(N) <= r b - r + 1, so at most min(v, r b - r + 1) - 1 efficiencies differ from 1, with the convexity argument of `block_a_bound()`; a start attaining it is scored once and returned without search | Own derivation (controller ruling R18, 2026-11-19); John & Williams (1995) ch. 2 (efficiency factors); nested-engine spec section 6.2b decision 4 |
| `R/engine_mols_data.R` (`stored_mols`), `tools/search-mols.R` | Orthogonal Latin square pairs of orders 14, 18 and 26, stored as data, and the seeded tool that found them: a random Latin square by a Jacobson-Matthews walk, then (method 1) its transversals by backtracking and q disjoint ones by Algorithm X, (method 2) cycle-switch local search on the mate, (method 3) backtracking for a pair developed over Z_(q-1) with one fixed point. Each pair is checked (both squares Latin, all q^2 ordered pairs different) before it is written | Own computer search, `tools/search-mols.R`; orthogonal pairs exist by Bose, Shrikhande & Parker (1960), Canad. J. Math. 12:189-203. Algorithms from the literature, no implementation consulted: Jacobson & Matthews (1996), J. Combin. Des. 4:405-437 (random Latin squares); Knuth (2000), "Dancing links" (Algorithm X); Dénes & Keedwell (1974), *Latin Squares and their Applications* (transversals and orthogonal mates, cycle switches); van Lint & Wilson, *A Course in Combinatorics*, ch. 22 (orthogonal arrays developed over a group; the difference conditions of method 3 are our own derivation). No pair was copied from a published table; nested-engine spec section 6.7 |
